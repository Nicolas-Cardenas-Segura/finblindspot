Finance Blind Spot

One build spec: what the customer sees, where each answer goes, what the engine does with it, the words behind every blind spot, and how the whole thing repeats in six months.

Version 1, frozen for the hackathon build. Content owner: Elena. Builders: Luca and Nicholas. This is the master build document. Nothing new goes in without agreeing it first, and anything thought of from here goes to v2.

1What the app is

A person answers around forty plain questions. The app gives them three things: an honest picture of where they stand, the gaps they had not noticed, and a saved position they can come back and update.

The re-assessment is the product, not a later feature. A one-off calculator gets used once and forgotten. A saved position that someone returns to in six months, updates in five minutes and sees moving is a thing they keep. Everything below is designed around that loop.

The loop. Assess, see your blind spots, act on your own terms, come back in six or twelve months, answer a prefilled form, see what moved.

Three separations that keep it safe and simple

  • The AI never does the maths. All numbers come from deterministic backend code. The AI asks follow-ups, explains results, and nothing else.
  • The questionnaire is the customer's view; field IDs are the contract. Every question maps to one ID. The engine only ever reads IDs.
  • "I don't know" is an answer, not a zero. It is stored as null, it never silently becomes 0, and on its own it produces a blind spot. Not knowing is the finding. The full input rule is in section 5.

What the AI may and may not do

May: ask follow-up questions, clarify an answer, point out missing information, explain a calculation or a concept, explain why something is a blind spot, suggest questions to ask a professional.

May not: recommend an investment, product or pension transfer, invent a missing value, present an assumption as a guarantee, or state that a projection will happen.

4How an assessment is stored

This is the part that makes the six-month re-run work, so it belongs in v1 even though nothing on screen shows it.

assessment { id user_id created_at status draft | complete base_currency answers { ...every field ID above } assumptions { inflation_rate, investment_growth_rate, cash_growth_rate, property_growth_rate, withdrawal_rate } derived { monthly_spending, monthly_surplus, net_worth, financial_assets } results { required_pot, projected_assets, position, position_today, extra_monthly, is_minimum_estimate, missing_fields[] } blind_spots [ { rule_id, severity, fired_at } ] }

Rules that follow from this shape:

  • An assessment is immutable once complete. A re-assessment creates a new record. Never overwrite, or the history the user came back for disappears.
  • Answers, assumptions and results are stored separately, so you can recompute an old assessment under today's assumptions.
  • Store which fields were null. "You didn't know your pension value last time, do you now?" is the single best prompt the app has.

5The calculation engine

Deterministic backend code. The user never sees a formula, only the output. Lump sums compound annually; monthly contributions compound monthly at investment_growth_rate / 12.

1 years = retire_age - age # must be >= 1 months = years * 12 mr = investment_growth_rate / 12 2 target_income_today = retire_income_monthly * 12 3 guaranteed_income_today = 12 * sum(pension_fixed_income_monthly where pension_start_age <= retire_age and the value is known) 4 income_needed_today = max(target_income_today - guaranteed_income_today, 0) 5 future_income_needed = income_needed_today * (1 + inflation_rate) ^ years 6 required_pot = future_income_needed / withdrawal_rate 7 projected_assets = sum(pension_value) * (1 + investment_growth_rate) ^ years + investments_total * (1 + investment_growth_rate) ^ years + max(cash_total - 3 * monthly_spending, 0) * (1 + cash_growth_rate) ^ years + property_equity_if_earmarked * (1 + property_growth_rate) ^ years + sum(pension_contribution_monthly where contributions continue) * (((1 + mr) ^ months - 1) / mr) + saving_monthly_other * (((1 + mr) ^ months - 1) / mr) - debt_at_retirement 8 position = projected_assets - required_pot 9 position_today = position / (1 + inflation_rate) ^ years 10 if position < 0: extra_monthly = -position * mr / ((1 + mr) ^ months - 1) 11 repeat steps 6, 8 and 9 at withdrawal_rate 3% and 5%

Three things in there that are easy to get wrong

  • Only cash above three months of spending is projected. An emergency fund is not a retirement asset, and counting it teaches the wrong lesson.
  • The two contribution lines are usually worth more than the starting balances. Someone saving €500 a month for 25 years builds around €298,000 at 5%. Omitting them turns almost every result into a false shortfall.
  • Property counts only when property_for_retirement = yes, and the home they live in never counts.

Zero, unknown and blank

Three different things. Never collapse them.

The user does thisStored asEngine behaviour
Answers "none", or enters 00A real value. Used in the maths, and can satisfy or fire a rule on its own terms.
Picks "I don't know" or "not sure"nullLeft out of the calculation, added to missing_fields, marks the result a minimum estimate, and fires the matching blind spot.
Leaves a required field blanknot storedPrompt them to answer, or to pick "I don't know" if they genuinely cannot. Do not submit and do not default it.

Never silently convert null to 0, in the engine or in the form. Every money and yes/no field must offer an explicit "I don't know", otherwise the user is forced to invent a zero and the blind spot is lost.

Missing information

If any field used above is null, leave it out, set is_minimum_estimate = true, list the missing fields, and fire the matching blind spot. Label the result on screen: "Minimum estimate based on what you know today. Missing: your UK pension value." Never substitute a guess, and never fail silently.

If retire_income_monthly is unknown, do not calculate at all. Show the blind spots instead, which are still useful on their own.

Validation

  • retire_age > age. If they are already there, switch to "what could my savings support now" rather than running a negative timeline.
  • Growth rates must be above zero. They are denominators, and a 0% rate divides by zero.
  • Money fields zero or positive.
  • pension_start_age between age and 90.
  • If guaranteed income already covers the target, show the no-gap message, not a negative pot.
  • Round everything displayed to the nearest hundred. False precision undermines an estimate.

Stated on screen as v1 simplifications

Tax is ignored. Rental income is ignored. Contributions are assumed flat. Fixed pension income is inflated at the same rate as everything else. One planning horizon, with no modelling of how long the money must last.

Tone rules for the copy

  • Observation, not verdict. "A large share of what you reported is in cash", not "you are making a mistake".
  • For cross-border wills: "your succession arrangements may be worth reviewing", never "your will is invalid".
  • Rule 20 asks a question rather than flagging an error. Wanting less in retirement is often deliberate.
  • Rule 17 needs a country-to-currency lookup table. Small, but somebody has to build it or the rule never fires.

The copy for all twenty, title, explanation, what to learn and the question to ask, is in section 7.

7Blind-spot content library

The words behind the twenty rules. One entry per rule: what the user sees, why it matters, what to go and learn, and the question to take to a professional.

How the builders use this

This is data, not code. Load it as a static file keyed by rule ID, so the copy can be corrected without a deploy and translated later without touching the engine.

blind_spot_content = { "thin_emergency_fund": { "title": "Your safety net is thin", "headline": "...", # one line, shown in the list "why": "...", # 2 to 3 sentences, why it matters "learn": ["...", "..."], # 2 to 3 concrete things to go and do or read "ask": "...", # one question to take to a professional "severity": "high", "topic": "savings" # matches a section H tick, for the severity bump }, ... }

The engine fires rule IDs. The AI layer may rephrase why around the user's own numbers, for example inserting their actual months of cover. It must not invent a new ask, change the learn items into product suggestions, or add a rule that did not fire.

The advice boundary is in the wording, and this file is where it is most easily broken. Every learn item describes something to find out, understand or check. None of them names a product, a provider or a transaction. Every ask is a question the user puts to a qualified person, which is the opposite of the app answering it.

Tone, applied to every entry below

  • Observation, not verdict. State what was reported and what it implies, not what they did wrong.
  • Second person, present tense, short sentences. No jargon that is not immediately explained.
  • "You told us" or "based on what you entered" whenever the finding rests on their own answer.
  • An unknown is a finding in its own right, and the copy says so plainly rather than treating it as a failure.
  • Never imply urgency to buy anything. The action is always to find out, understand or ask.

The twenty entries

Numbers match the rules table in the spec. Severity is the base value before the section H bump.

1Your safety net is thin High

thin_emergency_fund · cash_total < 3 × monthly_spending · topic: savings

Headline
You have less than three months of spending in cash.
Why it matters
Emergency money is what stops a bad month turning into a bad decade. Without it, a job loss, a medical bill or a broken boiler gets paid for with a credit card at 20%, or by selling investments at whatever price the market happens to offer that week. Everything else in your plan sits on top of this, which is why it is first.
What to learn
  • Work out your real monthly essentials, not your total spending. Rent, food, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments. That figure, times three to six, is the target.
  • Understand where cash can sit so it keeps some value against inflation but can still be reached within days.
  • Find out what your bank deposit protection scheme covers in the country where the money is held, and per what.
Question to ask a professional
"Given my job security and my dependants, how many months of essential spending should I hold in cash before I put money anywhere else?"

2You are spending more than you earn High

negative_surplus · monthly_surplus < 0 · topic: savings

Headline
What you told us you spend is more than what you told us you earn.
Why it matters
A negative monthly gap is filled from somewhere, usually savings or borrowing, and both work in the wrong direction. No investment return fixes a structural deficit, because the gap compounds faster than the portfolio. It is also the one number on this whole assessment you can change this month rather than this decade.
What to learn
  • Check the figures first. Mixing an annual bonus into a monthly income line, or the reverse, is the most common cause of this showing up wrongly.
  • Separate fixed commitments from discretionary spending, so you know how much is genuinely movable.
  • Understand which of your costs are contracted for a period and when each one next comes up for renewal.
Question to ask a professional
"My outgoings exceed my income. Which commitments should I look at first, and what would you want to see before I take on anything new?"

3People depend on you and there is no cover High

family_unprotected · dependants > 0 and life_cover ≠ yes · topic: protection

Headline
You have people who depend on your income, and no life cover recorded.
Why it matters
Everything else in this assessment assumes you keep earning. Life cover is the only part that works if you do not. For anyone with dependants and a mortgage it is usually the cheapest large risk to deal with, and the one most often left until later. If you answered "I don't know", that is worth resolving on its own: cover you cannot find is cover your family cannot claim.
What to learn
  • Check what you already have through your employer, and whether it survives you leaving that job. Most does not.
  • Understand the difference between cover that runs for a fixed term and cover that lasts for life, and what each is normally used for.
  • Work out roughly what your household would need: outstanding mortgage, plus the years of income that would need replacing.
Question to ask a professional
"If I died tomorrow, what would my household actually receive, and what is the gap between that and what they would need?"

4No cover if you cannot work Medium

no_income_safety_net · illness_cover ≠ yes · topic: protection

Headline
Nothing recorded that would replace your income if illness stopped you working.
Why it matters
Long-term illness is statistically more likely during your working life than death, and financially it can be harder, because the costs continue while the income stops. State support varies enormously by country and is often far below what people assume. Self-employed and cross-border workers tend to have the least of it.
What to learn
  • Find out what your employer pays if you are signed off sick, at what percentage of salary, and for how many months.
  • Find out what the state in your country of residence pays, and what contribution record it requires.
  • Understand the difference between cover that pays a lump sum on a listed diagnosis and cover that pays a monthly income for as long as you cannot work.
Question to ask a professional
"If I could not work for two years, what would I receive from my employer and from the state, and for how long?"

5No health cover recorded High

no_health_cover · health_cover ≠ yes · topic: protection

Headline
No health cover recorded where you live.
Why it matters
This one depends entirely on where you are, which is why the app asks rather than assumes. In some countries residence gives you comprehensive care and this is a non-issue. In others, or on certain visa types, a single serious episode is the largest bill a household will ever face. If you are living outside the country you pay into, check rather than assume.
What to learn
  • Confirm what your residence status entitles you to in the public system where you actually live, not where you are from.
  • If you have private cover, check what is excluded, what the annual limit is, and whether it covers treatment outside that country.
  • Check whether your cover follows you or ends when you change job or move.
Question to ask a professional
"On my residence status, what am I entitled to in the public system here, and what would I have to pay for myself?"

6Your succession arrangements may be worth reviewing Medium

succession_gap · will = no, or will_country ≠ residence_country, or will_year > 5 years old · topic: succession

Headline
Your will, or the lack of one, may not match where you now live.
Why it matters
Inheritance is governed by national law, and those laws differ sharply. Several countries apply forced heirship, where a fixed share must go to certain relatives regardless of what a will says. A will written in one country may still be valid but produce an outcome you did not intend in another. Without a will, the local default rules decide, and they rarely match how modern households are actually arranged.
What to learn
  • Find out which country's succession law applies to you, and whether you have any right to elect a different one.
  • Understand that assets in multiple countries can be subject to multiple regimes, and that one will does not always cover all of them.
  • Check whether anything major has changed since the will was written: a move, a marriage, a separation, a child, a property.
Question to ask a professional
"Which country's succession rules apply to my estate, and does my current will actually achieve what I intend under those rules?"

7Education costs are not funded Medium

education_unfunded · dependants > 0 and education_funded ≠ yes · topic: education

Headline
You have dependants and no confirmed plan for education costs.
Why it matters
Education is the one large expense with a date attached, which makes it both easier to plan for and harder to postpone. It also tends to land in the same years as peak retirement saving, so unfunded, it quietly takes the money that was meant for later. Education costs have historically risen faster than general inflation, so a figure that looks adequate today usually is not.
What to learn
  • Get a realistic number for the route you actually expect: local state, private, or university in another country, including living costs.
  • Work out the years remaining. Under five years is a very different problem from fifteen, and is generally treated differently.
  • Understand what fees look like for international or non-resident students, which are often several times the domestic rate.
Question to ask a professional
"Given the education route we expect and the years we have left, how much would we need to set aside each month, and how should that money be held?"

8You cannot see all your pensions High

pension_visibility · no pension rows, or any pension_value = null · topic: retirement

Headline
At least one pension has no value recorded, so your projection is a minimum.
Why it matters
For most people pensions are the largest single asset they own, and often the one they can describe the least. A pot you cannot value cannot be counted, so the projection above is lower than your real position, and you are planning against a number you know is incomplete. People who have moved countries or changed employers often have money sitting in schemes that have lost track of them entirely.
What to learn
  • List every employer you have had and check whether each one enrolled you in a scheme. A payslip from the time will usually show it.
  • Find out whether the country you worked in runs a pension tracing or dormant-accounts service, and use it.
  • For each pot, get three things: the current value, what it is invested in, and the age it can first be taken.
Question to ask a professional
"Help me build a complete list of my pensions across every country I have worked in, and tell me what each one is currently worth."
Boundary. This entry helps someone find and value their pensions. It never suggests moving, consolidating or transferring one. Transfer advice is regulated in most jurisdictions and stays off this app entirely.

9Your pension starts after you plan to stop High

pension_timing_gap · pension_start_age > retire_age · topic: retirement

Headline
You plan to retire at {retire_age}, and one of your pensions does not begin until {pension_start_age}.
Why it matters
Those in-between years have to be paid for from your own savings, with no pension arriving. It is the single most underestimated part of early retirement, and the reason people who retire a few years early run into trouble later rather than immediately. Because this projection stops at your chosen retirement age, the income arriving later is not counted at all, which makes the figure above deliberately conservative. It is your position at {retire_age}, not your position for the rest of your life.
What to learn
  • Confirm the exact age each pension can first be drawn. State pension ages have been rising in many countries and may not be what you remember.
  • Work out the cost of the bridge: the years between stopping and the pension starting, times your expected annual spending.
  • Understand whether taking a pension earlier than its normal age is possible where you are, and what permanent reduction applies if it is.
Question to ask a professional
"I want to stop at {retire_age} and my pensions start later. How would I fund those years, and what would it cost me to take anything early?"
Required copy. Whenever this rule fires, the headline result must be bounded by the retirement age and carry the conservative-estimate note. See section 8.

10Your pensions are spread across countries Medium

scattered_pensions · more than one pension_country · topic: cross_border

Headline
You have pensions in more than one country.
Why it matters
This is not a problem in itself, it is an admin and visibility risk. Each scheme has its own rules, its own claim process, its own start age and its own currency, and none of them will contact each other on your behalf. Pots go missing when people move, and schemes rarely chase a member across a border. The currency point matters too: a pension paid in one currency while you spend in another leaves your retirement income moving with the exchange rate.
What to learn
  • Keep one list: scheme, country, reference number, current value, start age, and how it is claimed.
  • Check what each scheme needs from you to pay out, and whether it can pay to an account in another country.
  • Find out whether there is a social security agreement between the countries involved, and whether contributions in one count towards entitlement in another.
Question to ask a professional
"I have pensions in several countries. What do I need to do in each one, and where will I be taxed on them when they pay out?"
Boundary. "Keep track of them" is the message. Not "bring them together".

11Nobody is named to inherit Medium

beneficiary_gap · beneficiaries_named ≠ yes · topic: succession

Headline
No beneficiaries recorded on your pensions and life cover.
Why it matters
Pensions and life policies often pass outside the will, under the scheme's own rules. That means an out-of-date nomination, or none at all, can send money somewhere you did not intend, and a will cannot always override it. It is one of the few gaps that costs nothing to close and can take ten minutes. A nomination made before a separation, or before a child, is the usual problem.
What to learn
  • Check each pension and each policy separately. They are nominated individually, not as a group.
  • Understand whether your scheme treats a nomination as binding or as an expression of wishes, because the difference matters.
  • Set a habit of reviewing nominations whenever your household changes.
Question to ask a professional
"Who is currently nominated on each of my pensions and policies, and does that match what my will says?"

12You do not know what you are paying in fees Medium

fees_unknown · fees_known ≠ yes · topic: investments

Headline
You told us you are not sure what your investments and pensions cost you each year.
Why it matters
Fees are the only part of investment returns that is known in advance, and they compound in exactly the same way as growth, just against you. On a pot growing at 5% over 25 years, the difference between paying 0.5% a year and 2% a year is roughly a third of the final value. Charges are usually taken from inside the fund, so they never appear as a payment you notice.
What to learn
  • Look for the total yearly cost, which usually means adding the platform or scheme charge to the fund charge, and any adviser fee.
  • Understand the difference between a one-off charge on money going in and an annual charge on the whole balance. The annual one is what compounds.
  • Try the same projection at your current cost and at one percentage point lower, so the effect is a number and not a principle.
Question to ask a professional
"What is the total annual cost of each of my investments and pensions, all charges included, as a percentage and in euros?"

13Expensive or unmeasured debt High

expensive_debt · debt_max_rate > 8% or null, with debt_total > 0 · topic: debt

Headline
You have debt costing more than a portfolio is likely to earn, or a rate you are not sure of.
Why it matters
This projection assumes investments grow at 5% a year and that is not guaranteed. Interest on a card at 20% is guaranteed. Money used to clear it returns that rate, with certainty and no market risk, which is a better deal than almost anything else available. If you answered "I don't know" for the rate, that is worth resolving first, because the rate determines whether this is urgent or routine.
What to learn
  • List every debt with its rate, so you can see them ranked rather than as one lump.
  • Understand which rates are fixed and which can be changed by the lender with notice.
  • Check for early repayment penalties before clearing anything, particularly on fixed-term loans.
Question to ask a professional
"Given these rates, should I be repaying debt before investing more, and in what order?"

14Debt is still running in retirement Medium

debt_into_retirement · debt_at_retirement > 0 or null · topic: debt

Headline
You expect to still owe money when you stop working.
Why it matters
Repayments do not stop when the salary does. Debt carried into retirement has to be serviced from the same pot that funds your living costs, so it reduces your income twice over: once through the repayments, and again through the capital you had to keep back. It can also limit your choices, because it is harder to borrow or refinance without employment income.
What to learn
  • Check the final payment date on each loan and mortgage, and compare it to your intended retirement age.
  • Work out what clearing the balance before you stop would require each month, and whether that is realistic.
  • Understand what happens to any interest-only balance at the end of its term.
Question to ask a professional
"I expect to still owe this at retirement. Should I be clearing it first, and what does it do to the income I can take?"

15A large share of your money is in cash Low

cash_concentration · cash_total ÷ financial_assets > 30% · topic: investments

Headline
More than a third of what you reported is sitting in cash.
Why it matters
Cash cannot fall in nominal terms, which is exactly why it feels safe, and it loses value quietly against inflation instead. At 3% inflation, money in an account paying 1% loses around a fifth of its purchasing power over ten years without the balance ever going down. Beyond your emergency fund and anything needed within a few years, holding it in cash is a decision with a cost, even though it does not look like one.
What to learn
  • Split the cash by purpose: emergency fund, money needed within three years, and the rest. Only the last part is really in question.
  • Compare the rate you are actually receiving with current inflation, rather than with zero.
  • Understand why money needed soon is normally kept in cash regardless of the inflation cost. Timing, not return, is the reason.
Question to ask a professional
"How much cash should I hold given my circumstances, and what should the rest be doing?"

16Most of your wealth is in property Low

property_concentration · net property ÷ total net assets > 60% · topic: property

Headline
Most of your net worth is tied up in property.
Why it matters
Property has served a lot of people well, and this is an observation about concentration rather than a criticism of the asset. Three things follow from it: you cannot sell a small part of a house when you need money, the value moves with one local market rather than many, and it costs money to hold through maintenance, tax and periods with no tenant. If the plan is to fund retirement by selling, the sale has to happen at a time that is not of your choosing.
What to learn
  • Work out your net position after mortgage, selling costs and any tax on gain, rather than the headline value.
  • Be honest about which properties you would actually sell, and which you would not.
  • Understand what property costs you annually to hold, as a percentage of its value.
Question to ask a professional
"If most of my wealth is in property, what would I actually live on, and how quickly could I access it if I needed to?"

17Your money and your life are in different currencies Medium

currency_exposure · cash_currency_mismatch = yes, or currency of retire_country ≠ base_currency · topic: cross_border

Headline
You hold money in one currency and expect to spend it in another.
Why it matters
Exchange rates move by 10% to 20% over a few years without anything unusual happening. If your savings are in one currency and your retirement will be spent in another, your real income moves with the rate, and you have no control over it. It is a risk that pays you nothing for taking it, which makes it different from investment risk. It is also the risk people who move countries notice last.
What to learn
  • List your assets by the currency they are actually held in, not the currency of the platform you see them on.
  • Do the same for the income you expect in retirement, then compare both to the currency you will spend in.
  • Understand the difference between an occasional conversion cost and a permanent structural mismatch. The second is the one that matters.
Question to ask a professional
"My assets are in one currency and I expect to retire in another. How exposed am I, and what are the normal ways of reducing that?"
Build note. This rule needs the country-to-currency lookup table from section 6, or it never fires.

18Only one of you knows where everything is Medium

single_point_of_failure · partner exists, decision_maker = me, partner_knows ≠ yes · topic: succession

Headline
You handle the money, and your partner does not know where it all is.
Why it matters
This is the most common gap in the whole assessment and the cheapest to close. If something happens to you, your partner deals with the worst weeks of their life while trying to find accounts they have never seen, with institutions that will not talk to them without paperwork they do not know exists. It is not about who makes the decisions. It is about whether the information survives one person being unavailable.
What to learn
  • Write one page: every institution, what is held there, and roughly how to reach it. Not passwords, just the map.
  • Make sure at least one other person knows that page exists and where to find it.
  • Understand what your bank or scheme requires from a partner in that situation, because it is usually more than people expect.
Question to ask a professional
"If I were not here, what would my partner need in order to access and manage all of this, and what should we put in place now?"

19There is a gap between the plan and the target High

retirement_gap · position < 0 at 4%, or is_minimum_estimate = true · topic: retirement

Headline
On today's assumptions, your projection falls short of the income you asked for.
Why it matters
A gap found now is a small monthly number. The same gap found ten years later is a large one, because the time left to fix it is what does most of the work. There are four levers and only four: save more, retire later, want less, or take different investment risk. Seeing which one moves the number most is more useful than the shortfall itself. If your result is marked a minimum estimate, the gap may be smaller than shown, and filling in what is missing is the first step rather than saving more.
What to learn
  • Try each lever one at a time in the assumptions panel and watch which one shifts the result most.
  • Understand what the withdrawal rate assumption means and why 3%, 4% and 5% produce such different answers.
  • Check whether the shortfall is real or an artefact of missing information, before changing any behaviour.
Question to ask a professional
"Are the assumptions behind this projection reasonable for my situation, and which change would close the gap most efficiently?"

20Is the lower retirement income deliberate? Low

lifestyle_reality_check · retire_income_monthly < 60% of monthly_spending · topic: retirement

Headline
The income you asked for is well below what you spend today. Is that intentional?
Why it matters
Often it is entirely deliberate: the mortgage will be gone, the children independent, the commute finished. Sometimes it is a figure typed quickly, and it makes the projection look far healthier than it should. The app cannot tell the difference, which is why it asks rather than flags. Worth knowing that some costs rise in retirement even as others fall, particularly healthcare, travel, and the time available to spend money in.
What to learn
  • Take today's spending and remove what genuinely ends: mortgage, commuting, costs of dependants, pension contributions.
  • Add what starts or grows: health cover, travel, help around the home later on.
  • Remember every figure here is in today's money, so you do not need to add inflation yourself. The engine does that.
Question to ask a professional
"Is this a realistic income for the retirement I am describing, in the country I plan to live in?"

On the re-assessment screen

A rule that fired last time and does not fire now is shown as closed, with the same title and a single line of confirmation. A rule that fired because of an unknown, and now has a real value, counts as closed too. That is the reward loop, and it is the reason someone comes back a third time.

What is deliberately not in the library

No product names, providers, platforms or funds. No numbers presented as targets beyond the illustrative ones used to explain a mechanism. No country-specific tax rates, allowances or thresholds, because they change annually and being wrong about them is worse than being silent. No instruction to transfer, consolidate, switch or buy anything. Where a topic is genuinely jurisdiction-dependent, the copy says "find out" and hands it to the question at the end of the entry.

  1. They get a nudge at six or twelve months, whichever they chose.
  2. The form opens prefilled with their last answers, one screen per section, each asking only "still right?" Anything they confirm is copied forward.

The one rule that makes the comparison honest

Recompute both assessments under the same assumptions before comparing them. If someone nudges growth from 5% to 6%, their position improves by a hundred thousand without them doing anything. Comparing stored result to stored result would show that as progress, which is a lie. Store answers and assumptions separately precisely so you can re-run the old answers under today's assumptions and show only real change.

If they did change an assumption, say so in one line: "this comparison uses your current assumptions for both dates."

What the engine must produce

delta = compare(previous.answers, current.answers, assumptions = current.assumptions) → position_change, net_worth_change, monthly_saving_change, emergency_months_change → blind_spots_closed[], blind_spots_new[], blind_spots_still_open[] → unknowns_resolved[] # answered "don't know" then, answered now

unknowns_resolved deserves its own line on screen. Finding out what your pension is worth is real progress, and no other finance app congratulates anyone for it.
